You're probably asking yourself "What?", "That's not the same thing", etc. But I assure you, clickbaity title aside, it is the cheapest way to make a CC Audio alternative that actually works on the Chromecast protocol.

I have this quite nice set of Panasonic bookshelf speakers, specifically the SA-PM03, they're an older model, from the 2000's, but unlike computers, phones, and other tech; good audio devices stay good. I set out to "modernise” them and make them support the Chromecast protocol, as it's the widest supported protocol, VLC, YouTube, MusicAssistant, Spotify, Tidal, you name it, it supports Chromecast. (If you're on Apple, most of these apps also use AirPlay, which is more open, has multiple open source server implementations, so you could use any old computer or a Raspberry Pi as a receiver, but that's beyond the scope of today's article.)

"wHy DoNt YoU BuY a ChRoMeCaSt AuDiO tHeN?”

Simply put, these things are scalped to hell and back, because they're still quite desirable.
At first glance, you'd question why, as it would appear they only do analog audio, and anyone who's building out a serious Hi-Fi would want digital audio as it's cleaner and can do surround sound and other such things.
It turns out, that not only does it do analog audio but also do TOSLINK digital audio, using the odd 3.5mm TOSLINK cables.
This makes them still very solid for Hi-Fi audio setups as they can (in theory, not researched) do surround audio, and of course output clean, high-res audio.
For ages they were the cheap option to add good quality network audio to an existing home audio setup.
That is until it was discovered by sellers on eBay/FB Marketplace/etc, who sell for $80 AUD or more. For that price you could have (before Google replaced it) purchased a Chromecast with Google TV! The RRP was placed at $59 AUD (for the 1080p model) and $99 AUD (for the 4K model)

What about the Chromecast Google TV?

You might presume because these are Android devices that they'd just work with a USB dock + Audio card; but rather these are quite picky, and I could not get mine to output audio to a USB DAC, it recognises the device (it says audio output is "External device” in Quick Settings, where it usually would say TV Audio). No matter what I poked, it was sending it over the HDMI Port, these do however support Bluetooth, and if your Hi-Fi has Bluetooth, that could work, but it sounds like HELL to try and re-connect it.

Discovery of The Solution

The Chromecast 1st Generation is quite an old device at this point, it released in 2013, and isn't super capable anymore due to a lot of streaming services having updated their receiver webapps. Even YouTube can't play at 1080p anymore. However, it still works very solidly for Audio.

I found this cheap HDMI to VGA adaptor I lying around, and when I connected it to my laptop, even with no monitor attached a new display pops up, which defaults to 480p, but can be set to up to 1080p.

The reason it simulates a display is because some older VGA displays did not properly define themselves to the attached device, this is why Windows' Display control panel has a "Detect older displays” button.

This cheap adaptor also happens to break out the audio from the Chromecast. I'm sure you can see where this is going.

The solution

By connecting the first-generation Chromecast to the VGA adaptor, it becomes a headless Chromecast with an audio jack, and it just works.

I don't have a newer one to test this for myself but I'd imagine it works just the same on any Chromecast, and should (I forgot to test this) work properly on the Google TV ones as well.
